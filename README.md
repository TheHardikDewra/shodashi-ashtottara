# Sri Shodashi Ashtottara Shatanama Stotram

Interactive guide to the 108 sacred names of Goddess Shodashi (Tripura Sundari) from the Brahmayāmala Tantra.

**Live site**: [shodashi-ashtottara.vercel.app](https://shodashi-ashtottara.vercel.app)

## What this is

The Sri Shodashi Ashtottara Shatanama Stotram is a sacred 108-name hymn to Goddess Shodashi (Tripura Sundari), preserved in the Pūrvakhaṇḍa of the Brahmayāmala Tantra. It is a dialogue in which Lord Brahmā, asked by sage Bhṛgu, reveals the 108 names of Sundarī - declared in the text itself to be extracted from the thousand names and described as *guhyād guhyataraṃ guhyaṃ* (secret, more secret than the secret, the very heart of secrecy).

This is the same stotra you hear playing as ambient devotion in **Lalita Maa Tripura Sundari's "world" inside the Tantra Sadhana app**.

This site is an open-source devotional offering for any seeker who wants to study, chant, or contemplate the 108 names with full Sanskrit text, transliteration, and meaning - on any device, online or offline.

## What's inside

- **All 108 divine names** with Devanagari, IAST transliteration, and English meanings
- **20 verses** total - Bhṛgu's request, Brahmā's response, 15 verses of names, and 3 phalashruti verses
- **Per-verse collective meanings** explaining the thematic clustering of each verse's names
- **Phala śruti** with optimal recitation times (niśītha-kāla, aruṇodaya, pradoṣa-kāla, navamī)
- **Namāvalī form** of every name (the "Śrī X namaḥ" form for individual recitation)

## Features

### Home
Welcome dashboard with stats, daily Name of the Day, sadhana tracker, framing verses (Bhṛgu and Brahmā's dialogue), phala śruti, and audio links.

### Verses
Navigate verse-by-verse through all 20 ślokas. Framing verses show the speaker; name verses show all their names with individual meanings plus the collective meaning; phalashruti verses show their full translation. Mark entire verses as learned. Progress bar tracks completion.

### Names
Browse all 108 names with search, filter by verse or name range, and learned/unlearned filtering. Click any name to expand and see: detailed meaning, verse it belongs to, the namāvalī "Śrī X namaḥ" form, and space for personal notes.

### Practice
Flashcard mode with spaced repetition (SM-2 algorithm). Two directions: Sanskrit-to-Meaning and Meaning-to-Sanskrit. Scope by verse, all/unlearned/due. Keyboard shortcuts: Space to flip, G for "got it", R for "review again".

### Chant
Full-screen teleprompter mode for daily recitation. Large Devanagari text, auto-advance with configurable speed, fullscreen API, keyboard navigation (arrows, space, F), optional transliteration and meanings overlay. Remembers your position between sessions.

## Data sources

- **Sanskrit (Devanagari) text**: [sanskritdocuments.org](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_devii/ShoDashIshatanAmastotram.html) - the volunteer-curated authoritative version of the full stotra (Bhṛgu's request, Brahmā's response, the 108 names in 15 verses, and the 3 phalashruti verses)
- **English meanings and verse-level reflections**: original work by the project author
- **IAST transliteration**: derived programmatically from Devanagari using a Devanagari→IAST converter

## Tech

Pure vanilla HTML / CSS / JS. No frameworks, no build step.

- Fonts: Haffer (local) > Geist > Inter for body; Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit for Sanskrit headings
- Dark theme default with light and system modes
- All progress persisted in localStorage
- Spaced repetition uses the SM-2 algorithm
- Data embedded in `data.js`, generated by `build_data.py`
- Service worker for offline support
- Deployed on Vercel

## A note for seekers

Per the text's own phala śruti, even a single recitation of this stotra with devotion is said to bring merit equal to ten million recitations of the Sahasranāma. The principle is *sāra* (essence): a distillation often carries greater potency than the diffuse whole, much as an attar concentrates the rose.

The Goddess instructs Bhṛgu that the optimal times for recitation are the four *sandhyā* points - the junctures where the kālacakra is most permeable: midnight (niśītha-kāla), dawn (aruṇodaya), evening twilight (pradoṣa-kāla), and the ninth lunar day (navamī).
